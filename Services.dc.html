Matrimonial investigations are professional services used to verify the claims, character, and activities of a prospective partner or a current spouse. These are broadly categorized into Pre-Matrimonial (preventative) and Post-Matrimonial (corrective) investigations.

1. Pre-Matrimonial Investigations

The goal here is to establish a foundation of trust before "tying the knot." It is especially common in arranged marriages or relationships initiated through matrimonial websites.

Key Focus Areas:

  • Background Verification: Verifying education, current employment, and actual salary/position.
  • Family Reputation: Checking the social and economic standing of the family in their neighborhood or community.
  • Character and Lifestyle: Investigating habits like smoking, drinking, drug use, or gambling, as well as checking for hidden past or current relationships.
  • Financial Health: Assessing assets, liabilities (loans/debts), and the general financial stability of the individual and family.
  • Medical History: Uncovering chronic illnesses or mental health issues that might have been concealed.
  • Criminal Records: Searching for any past or pending legal cases/FIRs.

2. Post-Matrimonial Investigations

These are typically conducted when trust has already been compromised within a marriage. They are often used as evidence for legal proceedings like divorce or alimony cases.

Key Focus Areas:

  • Infidelity (Adultery): Discreet surveillance to catch a cheating spouse, often involving photographic or video evidence.
  • Hidden Assets: Finding secret bank accounts, properties, or businesses, particularly during divorce settlements.
  • Abuse or Harassment: Documenting evidence of domestic violence or dowry related harassment.
  • Child Custody Support: Gathering evidence of a spouse's lifestyle or behavior to prove they are unfit for custody.
  • Suspicious Activities: Investigating sudden changes in routine, unexplained work hours, or secretive phone behavior.

How Investigations are Conducted

We use a mix of on ground work and digital tools:

  • Surveillance: Physical shadowing (tailing) the subject to document their daily routine and meetings.
  • Digital Footprints: Checking social media, online activities, and public records.
  • Discreet Inquiries: Interviewing neighbors, colleagues, or friends of friends without revealing our or the client's true identity.
  • Sting Operations: In complex cases, we may use undercover tactics to reveal the truth.

Corporate and employee background checks are a critical part of the hiring process and corporate due diligence. They involve verifying the accuracy of information provided by job candidates or assessing the integrity and reputation of a business, supplier, or partner.

1. Employee Background Checks (Pre-Employment Screening)

These checks are performed on prospective employees to mitigate risks like workplace violence, fraud, theft, and negligent hiring claims. The scope of the check usually depends on the job's seniority and sensitivity.

Key Verification Areas:

  • Identity and Address Verification: Confirming the candidate's legal identity and current/previous residential addresses.
  • Educational Credentials: Contacting institutions to verify degrees, diplomas, and dates of attendance to prevent resume padding.
  • Employment History: Confirming past job titles, dates of employment, and sometimes reasons for leaving. This helps identify undisclosed gaps in employment.
  • Criminal Records: Searching national, state, and local court records for convictions.
  • Reference Checks: Contacting provided references to gain insight into the candidate's work ethic and character.

2. Corporate Background Checks (Due Diligence)

These checks focus on the integrity, stability, and reputation of a company or its key executives, often before mergers, acquisitions, or significant investments.

Key Verification Areas:

  • Litigation History: Reviewing past and pending lawsuits involving the company, its subsidiaries, or its directors/officers.
  • Regulatory Compliance: Checking for regulatory fines, penalties, or violations issued by governing bodies.
  • Financial Stability: Analyzing public financial statements.
  • Reputation and Media Check: Searching public records, news articles, and social media for negative press, ethical issues, or controversies.
  • Corporate Structure and Ownership: Verifying the legal structure, main shareholders, and beneficial ownership to identify potential conflicts of interest.
  • Key Executive Integrity: Comprehensive background checks on the company's founders, CEOs, and senior management, similar to high level employee screening (criminal, financial, professional history).

Surveillance and evidence collection are the core of private and professional investigations. These processes involve the discreet observation of people, places, or activities to gather factual, undeniable proof that can be used in personal, corporate, or legal settings.

Objectives of Corporate and Employee Background Checks

The primary goal of background checks is risk mitigation and ensuring a trustworthy workforce.

  • Verifying Credentials: Confirming the accuracy of information provided on a resume or job application (employment history, educational degrees).
  • Workplace Safety: Identifying any history of criminal activity, violence, or substance abuse to protect existing employees and customers.
  • Financial Integrity: Checking credit history or financial misconduct for roles that involve handling money or sensitive financial data (accounting, executive positions).
  • Regulatory Compliance: Meeting legal and industry specific requirements, especially in regulated sectors like finance, healthcare, and government.
  • Preventing Fraud: Avoiding costly hiring mistakes, employee theft, or embezzlement.

Key Types of Background Checks

Background checks are tailored based on the job role and industry, but generally involve the following components:

  • Identity Verification: Confirms the candidate's legal identity using documents like a government ID (Aadhaar, Passport) and checks against national watchlists.
  • Address Verification: Confirms current and/or permanent residential addresses.
  • Employment History Verification: Contacting previous employers (often HR departments) to confirm job titles, dates of employment, and sometimes the reason for leaving or overall work performance.
  • Educational Verification: Contacting schools, colleges, and universities to verify the degrees, certifications, and dates of attendance listed by the candidate. This is crucial for roles requiring specialized qualifications.
  • Criminal Records Check: Searching local, state, and national public records databases for any history of convictions, arrests, or pending criminal cases.
  • Professional Licenses and Certifications: Verifying that professional licenses (doctors, lawyers, engineers) are valid, current, and have no sanctions or disciplinary actions against them.
  • Reference Checks: Contacting the professional references provided by the candidate to gain subjective insights into their work ethic, competence, and behavior.

The Verification Process (General Steps)

  • Conditional Offer: The company usually extends a conditional job offer first, contingent upon the successful completion of the background check.
  • Reporting and Review: A detailed report is compiled. The employer reviews the findings against their internal hiring criteria.
